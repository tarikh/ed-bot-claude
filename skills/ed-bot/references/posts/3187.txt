Today's SF Chronicle features the first installment in a three-part series by Mike Weiss on the scandal surrounding the James Beard Foundation,  which is "dedicated to furthering the practice and appreciation of the culinary arts in America."  Len Pickell, who had served the Foundation as volunteer, fundraiser, and eventually president, apparently spent more than $1 million on personal expenses.

But this isn't just your run-of-the-mill nonprofit mismanagement story.  The bizarre twist is that Pickell refused to accept a salary from the Foundation, claiming (falsely) that he was independently wealthy and didn't need the money!  Pickell was apparently a skilled administrator and charismatic fundraiser, and played a major role in the Foundation's growth over the past two decades.  He probably could have commanded a salary well over six figures and actually earned all the money he stole.  Instead, he couldn't escape his own lies, or perhaps he just found them thrilling.  Either way, it's a sad story with unhappy endings all around, not least for all the well-run nonprofits whose own fundraising will take a hit in the wake of one more scandal.

Pickell comes off as a likeable rogue, but the truth is that he abused the trust that all donors place in nonprofits, and other nonprofits will suffer as a result.  The scale of his misdeeds is piddling compared with the corporate scandals littering the news these days, but people don't stop investing when they hear about accounting tricks--they just move their money elsewhere (assuming they still have it.)  People do stop giving when they hear about nonprofit scandals.  Shame on him, and shame on a Board of Directors that was clearly asleep at the wheel.

UPDATE REDUX: Part Three in the series.  Money quote No. 1:

[Edna] Morris [,the Foundation's new Executive Director,] is getting $20,000 a month, plus the rent on a one-bedroom Greenwich Village apartment, where rents are typically $3,000 to $4,000. She is only in the job temporarily--to clean house, [former Board Chairman George] Sape says. With Beard picking up the tab, she continues to commute frequently to her home in Orlando.

All told, says one foundation insider who wants to remain anonymous but is voicing an oft-expressed view, "what she's getting is about the same amount Len was skimming."

And No. 2:

There remains the mystery of why Len Pickell  --  the son of dry cleaners, the househusband who doted on his daughters, the amateur oenophile  --  refused a salary, a six-figure salary, when the foundation he was building up  by leaps and bounds was willing to offer him one. Even his lawyer doesn't get that. "I said to his doctor," [attorney Stacey] Richman says, " 'Can you get me an answer to this question?' Because this is killing me."

A damn shame.
